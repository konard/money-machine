## How it works

Join networks such as Awin (absorbed ShareASale in 2025), CJ, Impact and Rakuten, get approved by merchants in one vertical (e.g. software, travel gear, home services), then generate many structured pages from a real dataset (specs, prices, features) with unique analysis per page. Merchants pay CPA/CPS commissions (typically 5-30%, cookie windows 30-90 days set per merchant).

Programmatic sites that win are built on proprietary or hard-to-gather data with real per-page analysis. Google's 2024-2025 scaled-content-abuse and site-reputation policies deindex thin template pages, and AI answers reduce clicks. Common failures: merchant approval refusals for new sites, then a traffic spike followed by a core-update collapse.

### Pricing strategy

Commissions are set by merchants; after you show volume, ask merchants for tiered or custom rates (often +2-5 points) and prioritise recurring or high-CPA offers.

### Path to profit

1. Month 0: pick a vertical, assemble a dataset, build templates, apply to 10-20 merchants (cost ~$50)
2. Month 1-3: publish 200-1,000 pages with unique analysis; expect $0-20
3. Month 3-6: prune pages without impressions, enrich winners; expect $20-100/month
4. Month 6-12: negotiate rates, add comparison tools; median ~$50/month, top sites $1k+

## What can be automated

- Ingest merchant product feeds/APIs into a database
- Template + LLM generate pages with unique per-entity commentary
- Auto-refresh prices, deals and dead links from feeds
- Track EPC per page and prune losers

## Unavoidable manual steps

- Network and merchant applications (often manual review)
- Quality review of generated pages
- Payment/KYC details per network

## Legality & compliance

Legal everywhere in the reference set; Google spam policies (scaled content abuse) are a ranking risk, not a legal one.

- **Compliance requirements:** affiliate disclosure (FTC/CAP); merchant program terms; tax forms per network
- **Platform availability:** Networks accept publishers from most countries; merchant approval is per program and often favours established sites. Some merchants only accept traffic from specific countries.

## Risks

- Merchant rejection for new sites
- Scaled-content penalties
- Commission reversals on returns
- Merchants leaving networks
