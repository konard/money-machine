## Realistic expectations

Most people who try this earn around **$0–$30 per month** in the first year; the high figure of $3,000 is a ceiling for a focused solo operator, not a typical outcome. The chance of receiving at least one real payout within six months is rated **4/10**. Expect the first payment after 30–240 d.

## How it works

Pick a narrow buyer-intent niche (e.g. tools for a specific hobby), publish genuinely useful comparisons, buying guides and spec tables, and link products with your Associates tag. Amazon pays a category-based % of purchases made within the 24-hour click window. Rates are low: Amazon US's official fixed table runs 0-10% by category (most categories 1-4.5%, e.g. 3% home/toys, 2.5% PC, 1% grocery/health), and 2026 trade press reports further cuts for some publishers, so volume and real first-hand testing are what differentiate a site from AI-generated competitors.

**Platforms:** Amazon Associates (local Amazon stores), WordPress/static site, Google Search

## Legality & compliance

Affiliate marketing is legal across the reference set; disclosure required. UK ASA/CAP guidance: affiliate content must be obviously identifiable as advertising (e.g. 'Ad' label upfront); a footer disclaimer or vague 'may earn a commission' wording is unlikely to be enough.

- **Compliance requirements:** affiliate disclosure (FTC/CAP); Amazon Operating Agreement; income tax / tax interview
- **Platform availability:** Anyone can join any Amazon store; direct deposit only to supported banks per store, otherwise gift card/check. Content must target countries with an Amazon store (US, UK, DE, FR, IT, JP, CA, AU, BR, MX, IN, SG etc.).

## Risks

- Amazon commission cuts and 24h cookie
- Account closure for policy breaches
- AI Overviews reducing click-through

## Evidence that people get paid

Amazon Associates is the largest affiliate program; official help confirms the ~60-day payout cycle and publishes the fixed category rate table (0-10%, mostly 1-4.5%). 2026 trade-press reporting describes rate cuts of up to 50% for some publishers, so expectations should be modest.
