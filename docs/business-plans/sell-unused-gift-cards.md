## How it works

Gift-card exchanges buy unwanted cards outright (CardCash pays up to about 92% of face value) or let you list them for sale to other buyers (GCX). You enter the card number, accept the offer and are paid by ACH, PayPal or cheque after verification. Raise.com now focuses on selling discounted cards to buyers and states it 'operates separately from the GCX Marketplace', where reselling happens. This is a one-off source of cash rather than income, but it is near-certain and useful for turning gift-card-only rewards from other programs into money in a bank.

**Platforms:** CardCash, GCX (Gift Card Exchange), Raise (buying side)

## Legality & compliance

Legal; exchanges perform fraud checks and require ID. Selling cards you did not legitimately obtain is fraud.

- **Compliance requirements:** ID verification; legitimately owned cards
- **Platform availability:** Primarily US (CardCash, Raise, GCX). UK has Cardyard; other countries have limited options.

## Risks

- Discount of 8–30% of face value
- Cards with zero balance are rejected and may trigger account closure
- US-only in practice

## Evidence that people get paid

CardCash and Raise have operated since 2009–2013 and are the standard way US consumers liquidate gift cards; both publish payout methods and timelines.
