# Remote incorporation options

Most people should start as a **sole proprietor in their own country** (see [incorporation costs by country](./incorporation-costs.md)); it is the cheapest, fastest and simplest way to invoice and pay tax. A company somewhere else only makes sense when a platform requires it (for example, Stripe or Paddle are not available at home), when clients insist on a US/UK/EU entity, or when the home regime is unusually costly.

Rule of thumb: a foreign company does **not** change where you pay tax. If you live and work in country X, X will usually tax the income (and may treat the foreign company as resident there). Get local advice before relying on any of these. All prices below are approximate, in USD, as of November 2026, and marked by confidence.

## Cheapest realistic paths

1. **Stay a sole proprietor at home** and use Payoneer or Wise to receive USD/EUR. Works in almost all 50 reference countries. Cost: usually $0–$50.
2. **UK Ltd** if a platform requires a company and your country lacks Stripe: ~$130 (£100) to form, ~$500/yr to maintain with a cheap accountant.
3. **US LLC via Stripe Atlas or a registered agent** if you need Stripe, Paddle, Mercury or US app marketplaces: ~$500 to form via Atlas (Delaware), then ~$500–$900/yr ($400 Delaware LLC tax + $100 agent + help with the mandatory Form 5472 filing); a Wyoming LLC costs less ($60 annual report minimum).

## What to check before choosing

- Does the platform accept a sole proprietor from your country? Check the [platform rules](../research/platform-rules.md) and the payout rails in `data/countries.json`.
- Will your home country tax the foreign company anyway (permanent establishment / controlled foreign company rules)? Usually yes.
- Can you open a bank or fintech account for the entity without travelling? Mercury (US) and Wise Business (UK/EU) are the common answers; both reject some countries. Wise does not serve residents of Afghanistan, Belarus, Burundi, Central African Republic, Chad, Congo, DR Congo, Cuba, Eritrea, Iran, Iraq, North Korea, Libya, Myanmar, Somalia, South Sudan, Russia, Sudan, Syria, Yemen, Venezuela or occupied Ukrainian regions; Mercury's list was not confirmed.
- Total yearly cost versus expected profit: a $700/yr structure is pointless for a plan whose median income is $20/month.
