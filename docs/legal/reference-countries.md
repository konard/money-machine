## 1. Reference set: Freedom on the Net (FOTN) 2025, Free + Partly Free

### Which edition and why

- **FOTN 2025** covers June 2024 to May 2025 and assessed **72 countries**. It is the latest _full_ edition,
  and it is the edition used here. [V]
- **FOTN 2026**, _Seismic Shifts on the Global Internet_, launched on 22 Oct 2026. Budget cuts reduced it to
  **38 countries**, so it cannot define a 50-country set. [V]
  - The only status change it reports is **Georgia, Free 70 -> Partly Free 68**. Georgia stays in the set either way.
  - Other 2026 data points: Kenya 58 -> 59, and the United States unchanged at 73. [V]
  - Bangladesh and The Gambia improved; Jordan and Nigeria declined. None of them crossed the 40-point line
    as far as the press release says. [V]
- FOTN status bands: Free = 70–100, Partly Free = 40–69, Not Free = 0–39.

**Result: 50 reference countries (18 Free + 32 Partly Free). 80% = 40 countries.**

## 2. Activity legality across the 50 reference countries

### How to read the categories and percentages

- **legal**: residents may lawfully do the activity through a normal private or licensed channel.
- **restricted**: the activity is lawful only through a state monopoly or a narrow channel, or it carries
  heavy legal constraints such as a payment-use ban, foreign-exchange (FX) controls, or registration required
  before starting.
- **banned**: the activity is prohibited for residents.

Each class gets two percentages:

- **Strict %** = legal / 50. **Use this one for the ">= 80% legal" gate.**
- **Lenient %** = (legal + restricted) / 50.

### (a) Cryptocurrency: owning, trading, and being paid in it

**Banned (5):**

- **Bangladesh**: Bangladesh Bank has repeatedly declared crypto transactions illegal (2014, 2017, 2022) under
  FX and anti-money-laundering law. [V via Wikipedia]
- **Morocco**: Office des Changes ban since 2017. A crypto-asset bill (Law 42.25) was drafted in 2025;
  treat the ban as in force until that bill is enacted. [V for the 2017 ban, K for the bill]
- **Tunisia**: the central bank (BCT) and FX law treat crypto as prohibited. [K]
- **Libya**: Central Bank of Libya ban, 2018. [K]
- **Iraq**: Central Bank of Iraq ban, 2017, reaffirmed 2022. [K]

**Restricted (4):**

- **Indonesia**: legal to hold and trade as a commodity (Bappebti, now regulated by the financial-services
  authority OJK), but **banned as a means of payment** by Bank Indonesia (2018). [V via Wikipedia]
- **Ecuador**: legal to hold and trade, but banned as payment. [V via Wikipedia]
- **Cambodia**: the National Bank of Cambodia bars banks from dealing in crypto, and only licensed providers
  may operate. [V via Wikipedia]
- **Jordan**: banking ban since 2014. A licensing law for virtual assets was passed in 2025. [V for the
  banking ban, K for the 2025 law]

**Legal, with notes:**

- India: legal, but taxed at 30% plus 1% tax deducted at source (TDS).
- Nigeria: the 2021 banking ban was lifted in Dec 2023, and the Investments and Securities Act 2025 now
  regulates crypto through the SEC.
- Angola: crypto mining was banned in 2024, but holding and trading are not banned.
- Argentina and Colombia: banks may not facilitate crypto, but individuals may hold and trade.
- Kenya and Ghana: virtual-asset service provider (VASP) acts were passed in 2025.

**Source:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legality_of_cryptocurrency_by_country_or_territory [V, partly out of date]

### (b) Online gambling, betting, and prediction markets

- **India**: the Promotion and Regulation of Online Gaming Act 2025 bans online real-money games nationwide. [K]

### (f) Receiving foreign-currency payments through PayPal, Stripe, Wise and Payoneer

#### Legality

Receiving payment for exported services is lawful in 49 of the 50 countries. Some impose repatriation or
reporting rules, for example India's FEMA purpose codes and Bangladesh's export-proceeds rules.

- **Restricted:** Tunisia. Residents need central-bank (BCT) authorization to hold foreign-currency
  accounts abroad, so balances held with Payoneer or PayPal are a grey area. [K]

### (g) Bandwidth-sharing apps (Honeygain, Pawns.app, EarnApp, Grass, and similar)

- No reference country specifically prohibits residents from sharing spare bandwidth, so legal coverage is 100%. [K]
- Risks are contractual and practical, not legal:
  - Many ISPs' acceptable-use policies forbid reselling or sharing the connection.
  - Your IP address can be used by third parties, which creates abuse-attribution risk.
  - Countries that regulate VPNs and proxies (India's CERT-In logging rules, for example) target providers,
    not end users.
- Platform availability and payout rails (PayPal or crypto) are the binding constraint. Crypto payouts are
  unusable in the 5 crypto-banned countries.

## 3. Machine-readable file

`countries.json` (same directory) maps each ISO-2 code to:

- `name`, `fotnScore`, `status`
- `paypalReceive`: `true` / `false` / `"unknown"`
- `stripeSupported`, `wiseSupported`, `payoneerSupported`: booleans
- `cryptoLegal`, `gamblingOnlineLegal`: `"legal"` / `"restricted"` / `"banned"`

Its values match the lists above.
